THE MATHEMATICS OF LISTENING

How it works

The primes appear irregular. Beneath that surface is a structure you can hear.

RIEMANN’S EXPLICIT FORMULA

$$\psi(x) = x - \sum_\rho \frac{x^\rho}{\rho} - \log 2\pi - \tfrac12\log(1-x^{-2})$$

The nontrivial zeros describe oscillations in the distribution of primes. Here, those oscillations become a musical score.

01 / PITCH

Pitch from the zeros

Each imaginary part γk becomes a frequency:

$$f_k = f_{base}\cdot\gamma_k/\gamma_1$$

The first zero, γ₁ ≈ 14.1347, maps to C3 at 130.81 Hz. The others form an inharmonic spectrum: related by mathematics, without settling into a conventional chord.

02 / WEIGHT

Loudness from Riemann’s weights

$$A_k = 1/\sqrt{0.25+\gamma_k^2}$$

Higher zeros speak more softly. Their amplitude follows the magnitude of the reciprocal zero, the weight that appears in the explicit formula.

03 / SPACE

A chord that slowly opens

The first 100 voices enter as the camera passes their zeros. Each fades in over 3.5 seconds, with its own slow amplitude modulation, stereo position, and gently detuned chorus. A widening filter and long reverb let it unfold.

04 / RHYTHM

The primes become bells

$$T_p = 1 + 0.15p$$

Every prime through 1093 strikes an FM bell. The gaps between primes become gaps in time. Twin primes, two units apart, create a distinctive double strike, always 0.30 seconds apart. The second bell is brighter and answers from the opposite side.

05 / THE JOURNEY

Up the critical line

Beyond the first 100 zeros, another 900 appear as short glints, folded by octaves into a comfortable register. We climb to Im(s) ≈ 1419 before the view widens and the sound recedes.

The field uses domain colouring of a zero-product approximation with a Riemann–Siegel θ phase. Contours wind around the zeros; particles gather at sounding wells; prime bells send ripples across the strip. This is an artistic rendering, not the exact ζ function.

06 / WHY NOW

A proof, announced

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge released a formal proof of the Riemann Hypothesis written in the Lean proof assistant. The Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative have certified that it compiles and states the canonical hypothesis; independent checks and a human-readable account are still in progress.

The 1,000 zeros in this piece were verified to lie on Re(s) = ½ long ago, numerically. If the proof stands, every nontrivial zero does: the chord never leaves the line.
